A Swedish activewear brand on a headless storefront

Stronger sells activewear from Stockholm on a Centra and React Router storefront, with content in Storyblok. I joined through TRY in September as the frontend between the brand's e-commerce team and the dev agency that runs the storefront.

strongerlabel.com in September, before the first test went live.

The product page already carried four earlier test winners; the view counter would have been the fifth element competing for the same screen.

The ask was a view counter

The brief that reached me was small: improve the "people browsing" pill on the product page. The test history said why not. Ten experiments, nine of them on the product page, four winners stacked on the same screen. The product list, the size picker and the mini-cart had never been tested.

Walking the store as a shopper next to Gymshark, AYBL and Aim'n gave four gaps, and each gap became a test with one hypothesis, one primary metric and an exposure point.

The bestseller badge, first out

The product list had no signal for what sells. Shoppers chose between forty near-identical tights with nothing but price to go on. The badge reuses the slot the Archive badge already had in the card's top corner, so the grid never shifts, and it follows the colour variant rather than the product family.

It looked like a one-day job until I traced where the flag lived: the product page reads a Centra attribute, the list cards read Voyado Elevate, so a feed mapping came first. Goal add to cart, target +5 %.

Figma, ships state: mobile card, laptop card, 2-col grid.

Control and variant from production, same session. Badge style GT America 500 12/15 on #18181b.

The badge went live on 20 October

"Bästsäljare" on the product-list cards, 50/50 on a user cookie, GA4 in BigQuery, Bayesian with CUPED. Five days in, about 18,000 users per arm: bestseller card clicks +4.4 %, other cards +2.2 %, add to cart +1.1 % with a wide interval.

The click lift looked like a win by day two; the goal is still flat, so it runs the full two weeks. Final read 3 November.

Accessibility fixes along the way

Between tests, the storefront got the accessibility work it had been missing: keyboard reach of the header and the cart, focus handling in the cart drawer, and the gaps that still remain, documented so they can be closed.
